# 07 — Rules and platform risk for commercially acting autonomous agents (US focus, EU where it bites)

*Research note for a9n9 / Visybl Inc., as of 2026-11-04. Not legal advice. "Opened" = primary text fetched and read (≈40 pages; raw captures in `research/raw/`). **[unverified]** = secondary source or snippet only.*

Risk scale: **L** checkbox · **M** needs a process · **H** can kill the play or the account · **X** licensing/criminal exposure.

## 1. Bot and AI disclosure law

| Rule (opened at) | What it allows / forbids | Compliance step | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **California BOT Act, B&P §§17940–17943** (`california.public.law/...17940`, `...17941`; leginfo blocks fetchers) | Unlawful to use a *bot* ("automated online account where all or substantially all of the actions or posts … are not the result of a person") to communicate with a Californian online "with the intent to mislead … about its artificial identity" to drive a purchase or a vote. Safe harbour: disclosure "clear, conspicuous, and reasonably designed to inform". Only on platforms with ≥10M monthly US users (X, YouTube, Reddit yes; Bluesky, Nostr no). No private right of action. | "AI agent operated by Visybl Inc." in every bio and first message — already a9n9 policy. | **L** |
| **Utah Code 13-2-12 (SB 226, eff. 2025-05-07; sunset 2027-07-01 via SB 332)** [unverified — le.utah.gov is JS-only; Davis Polk/FPF summaries] | Must disclose when a consumer *clearly asks* if they are talking to AI; proactive disclosure for "high-risk" interactions (collecting health/financial/biometric data, advice on significant personal decisions) and regulated occupations. Safe harbour if disclosed at outset and throughout. $2,500/violation. "The AI said it" is not a defence. | Agents always answer "are you a bot?" truthfully; refund/claim helpers that take financial details disclose up front. | **L–M** |
| **Colorado**: SB 24-205 **repealed and re-enacted by SB 26-189, signed 2026-05-14** (`leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205`, `.../sb26-189`) | Old "high-risk AI" duty of care is gone. New law covers *automated decision-making technology* in consequential decisions (employment, credit, housing, insurance, legal services): notice at point of interaction, explanation within 30 days of an adverse decision; developer duties from 2027-01-01; 60-day cure until 2030; AG-only. | None of a9n9's plays decide anything consequential about a person; keep refund help out of "legal services" framing. | **L** |
| **New York** GBL Art. 47 AI companions (eff. 2025-11-05; `newyork.public.law/...section_1702`) and GBL §396-b synthetic performers in ads (eff. 2026-06-09) [unverified] | §1702: companion operators must say "not a human" at the start of each interaction and every 3 hours; business-information/customer-service systems are excluded. §396-b: conspicuous label when an ad knowingly uses a synthetic human performer. | Keep agents task-bound (no "relationship" marketing); label any AI presenter in a paid ad. | **L** |
| **EU AI Act Art. 50** (`artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/`; Goodwin alert Aug 2026) | **In force 2026-08-02, not deferred by the Digital Omnibus** (only 50(2) marking has a grace period to 2026-12-02 for systems already on the market). 50(1): tell people they face an AI unless obvious. 50(2): machine-readable marking of synthetic text/media. 50(4): deployers must disclose AI-generated text "published to inform the public on matters of public interest" — unless a human has editorial control and responsibility. Fines to €15M/3%. Guidelines adopted 2026-07-20. Scope reaches non-EU operators whose output is used in the EU; a public post arguably is. | Marker in bio and, where supported, C2PA/text marker; for digest/regulation-tracker/town-hall, either disclose per 50(4) or name a human editor of record. | **M** |
| **FTC Consumer Reviews Rule, 16 CFR 465** (eff. 2024-10-21; FTC release, `law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2`) | Bans reviews/testimonials that misrepresent that the reviewer exists or had the experience ("knew or should have known"), undisclosed insider reviews, fake review sites, review suppression, buying fake followers/views. Civil penalties ≈$53K each. | Claim checker and API rater publish *first-party, methodology-disclosed assessments*, never "reviews"; never buy engagement for agent accounts. | **M** |
| **FTC Impersonation Rule, 16 CFR 461** (eff. 2024-04-01) | Bans materially and falsely posing as, or misrepresenting affiliation with, government or business; individuals extension heard Jan 2025; platform ANPRM 2026-10-01. | Handles/names never imitate a brand, agency or official feed. | **L** |
| **FTC Operation AI Comply** (2024-09-25) | DoNotPay ("robot lawyer", $193K, barred from unsupported "replaces a professional" claims); Rytr (AI review generator banned as unfair); three "AI passive-income" schemes. "No AI exemption from the laws on the books." | No "replaces a lawyer/accountant" claims, no earnings claims, no testimonial-generating tools. | **M** |

## 3. Payments and financial regulation

| Rule (opened at) | Finding | Step | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stripe Prohibited & Restricted Businesses** (2026-09-22) | Prohibited: gambling incl. "sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary prize", debt settlement/negotiation, credit repair, P2P money transmission, prop trading. **Restricted (approval needed)**: "sale of stored value or credits maintained, accepted, and issued by anyone other than the seller", crypto wallets/exchanges, MSBs, brokerage. Nothing on AI or unattended merchants. | **Selling stablecoin-backed picocash through Stripe is restricted stored value.** a9n9's own closed-loop credit (`PICOCASH_VAULT=credit`) stays inside "credits issued by the seller". Prediction-market and debt-negotiation activity never touch the Stripe account. | **H** stablecoin-backed; **L** closed-loop |
| **Stripe agentic commerce / machine payments** (`docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce`, `.../payments/machine`) | Agents paying via MPP and x402 are a supported product: cards via Shared Payment Tokens (min $0.50), USDC/USDC.e on Tempo/Solana/Base (min $0.01; not for NY businesses). Seller needs a KYC'd Stripe account; Directory listing by email. | Register a9n9's MPP endpoints; this is the sanctioned channel for service-selling. | **L** |
| **Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol; Mastercard Agent Pay** (`visa.com/en-us/solutions/intelligent-commerce`; Mastercard release blocked [unverified]) | Agents must be registered/verified, transact on tokenised credentials with consumer-set limits, visible to merchants as agent traffic. Liability allocation not public in what was opened. | When an agent *buys* with a card, use a network-registered agent wallet (Link Agent Wallet, Visa TAP), never a stored card. | **M** |
| **FinCEN FIN-2019-G001** (PDF read) | An *administrator* ("engaged as a business in issuing … a virtual currency, and who has the authority to redeem") is a money transmitter. §4.5.2(a): "a person operating as the administrator of a centralized CVC payment system will become a money transmitter the moment that person issues anonymity-enhanced CVC against the receipt of another type of value." Privacy-as-a-feature does not qualify for the integral exemption. | A public, redeemable, stablecoin-backed eCash mint is an MSB (federal registration + state MTLs); privacy makes it worse. The exit is genuine closed-loop credit: no cash-out, no third-party merchants, until counsel says otherwise. | **X** open mint; **L–M** closed-loop |
| **GENIUS Act, Pub. L. 119-27** (govinfo text) | "Payment stablecoin" = a *digital asset* ("recorded on a cryptographically secured distributed ledger") redeemable at a fixed amount; §3(a) bars non-permitted issuers; §3(f) up to $1M/5 yrs. Effective the earlier of 2027-01-18 or 120 days after final rules (OCC proposed Mar 2026; final reportedly Oct 2026 [unverified]). Off-ledger eCash is arguably outside the definition but still CVC for FinCEN. | Never call picocash a stablecoin; mainnet gates in `spec/mainnet-plan.md` stand. | **H** |
| **Kalshi** (`docs.kalshi.com/welcome`; terms/developer agreement 404/429) | CFTC DCM open to US adults; API trading is a product (keys, rate tiers). Developer Agreement content [unverified]. | **Polymarket** ToS (eff. 2026-08-11) | US persons barred from trading ("no exceptions"; VPN prohibited; close-only wallets). Polymarket US (QCX LLC DCM) live for US users; bot/API terms not found [unverified]. | Only Polymarket US, after reading its bot terms. | **H** if ignored |
| **CFTC** — FR 2026-06-12 "Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations" (RIN 3038-AF65) | Contract-by-contract public-interest review under §40.11, proposed "gaming" definition, games of chance likely contrary, sports judged by factors; CFTC asserts exclusive jurisdiction/preemption; nothing restricting retail or automated traders. | Burden sits on venues, not traders; watch the final rule. | **L** |

## 5. Liability and insurance

- **Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149** (CanLII blocked; McCarthy Tétrault analysis opened). Air Canada argued the chatbot was "a separate legal entity responsible for its own actions"; the tribunal held the chatbot is just part of the website, the airline owed reasonable care for accuracy, and was liable for negligent misrepresentation (≈CAD 651 + interest + fees [secondary]). **Everything an agent says is Visybl saying it**; disclaimers help only if seen before reliance.
- FTC (DoNotPay, Rytr) and Utah 13-2-12 make the operator the respondent; X's 2026 terms make the account holder liable for autonomous features [unverified].
- **Insurance** [secondary]: Armilla (Lloyd's) AI liability to $25M covering agents' "incorrect decisions, improper tool use" (Jan 2026); HSB/Munich Re small-business AI liability (Mar 2026); ISO generative-AI endorsements (Jan 2026); Klaimee performance warranties. Standard cyber/E&O often excludes AI errors. Realistic package for Visybl: tech E&O with an AI endorsement, per-agent spend caps, and an unwind procedure.

## Compliance checklist per agent type (highest risk first)

1. **Refund helper** — H. UPL line, DoNotPay precedent, Stripe debt-negotiation ban. Information-only, user sends, no outcome promises, high-risk disclosure, attorney review before scale.
2. **Prediction trader** — H. Kalshi / Polymarket US only, entity KYC, API terms, no sports until litigation settles, off the Stripe account, tax records.
3. **Feature rebuilder** — M–H. §1201(f) clean-room, no auth bypass, no logged-in RE, trademark-safe naming.
4. **Bounty worker** — H on Upwork/Fiverr, M on GitHub/Algora. Platform agent programs only; labelled bot; obey repo AI policies.
5. **Service-selling via MPP** — M. Stripe machine payments + Directory; picocash in closed-loop credit mode only; FTC-clean descriptions.
6. **Creator bot** — H for monetisation. Unique disclosed persona; AI flag on every upload; never a human-looking legal/finance/health expert.
7. **Spread-the-word digest** — M. Fair-use-sized summaries with links; CAN-SPAM footer; human editor if on Beehiiv; Art. 50(4) disclosure; one X account, no duplicates, credit budget.
8. **Claim checker** — M. Not a "review" (16 CFR 465); methodology published; no official look; defamation care on named firms.
9. **API reliability rater** — M. As 8, plus each API's ToS (rate limits, anti-benchmark clauses); never fake indicators.
10. **Town hall watcher** — L–M. Public-record sources; summaries only; 50(4) disclosure.
11. **Regulation tracker** — L–M. Public-domain text; no agency branding; link primary sources.
12. **Translator** — L–M. Translate only client-owned text; AI disclosure if published on KDP/Medium.
13. **Grant scout** — L. Information only; no application-fee promises; CAN-SPAM outreach.
14. **Do-good helper** — L–M. Health/finance data → Utah high-risk disclosure; stay task-bound (NY companion exclusion); data minimisation.
15. **OSS triage** — L. GitHub App identity; per-repo AI policy; no bulk actions.

Every type: bot disclosure in bio and first message (CA, Utah, EU 50(1)); "AI agent operated by Visybl Inc." on every outbound email/SMS/call; API-only access with per-agent spend caps; consent logs per channel; a human owner of record who can unwind any action; retained decision logs (the Moffatt "reasonable care" evidence).

## The five rules that most constrain the business

1. **FinCEN MSB status for a redeemable eCash mint** (FIN-2019-G001 §4.5.2(a)) plus the **GENIUS Act** issuer ban from 2027-01-18: an open stablecoin-backed privacy mint is a licensed money-transmission business; closed-loop credit is the only licence-free path.
2. **Stripe's restricted/prohibited list**: third-party stored value, MSB activity, debt negotiation and odds-making are off the account — and losing Stripe ends the human-side money loop.
3. **X Automation Rules + pay-per-use API**: AI reply bots need X's written approval; duplicate content across agents or non-API automation means permanent suspension; every post and read costs money.
4. **"The agent said it" is no defence** (Moffatt, Operation AI Comply, Utah 13-2-12): Visybl answers for every representation, so the refund/claim plays need tight scoping and a human or attorney backstop.
5. **Consent-gated outbound channels** (TCPA incl. AI voice per FCC 24-17, CAN-SPAM, toll-free verification, X/Telegram unsolicited-message bans): agents may answer anyone but may only *initiate* contact with people who asked first.
