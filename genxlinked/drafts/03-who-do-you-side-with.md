Most Gen Xers know the feeling. Your parent rings with an opinion about your kid's job, haircut or phone habits. An hour later your kid sends a message about your parent that you are definitely not forwarding. You are the switchboard.

There is a name for this position: the sandwich generation. And it is not a small club.

## How many of us are in the middle

In the United States, Pew Research Center defines being "sandwiched" as having a living parent aged 65 or older while also raising a child under 18 or having financially supported an adult child in the past year. In its latest update, Pew found that [a quarter of US adults are now in that position](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2022/04/08/more-than-half-of-americans-in-their-40s-are-sandwiched-between-an-aging-parent-and-their-own-children/), up from 23% when it last measured in 2021.

The middle-aged are where it concentrates. More than half (54%) of Americans in their 40s are sandwiched, and so are 45% of those in their 50s, up from 36% in 2021. That is Gen X almost exactly.

It is not all strain. In Pew's 2021 data, sandwiched adults were slightly more likely than others to say they were very satisfied with their family life (48% against 43%).

In the UK, the Office for National Statistics counts "sandwich carers": people who look after a sick, disabled or older relative while also caring for dependent children. A bulletin it published in November 2024 estimated [1.4 million sandwich carers aged 16 to 64](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/healthandsocialcare/socialcare/bulletins/sandwichcarersuk/january2021tomay2023) in the UK between 2021 and 2023. Around half (51%) were aged 45 to 64, and 61% were women. It is a heavy load: about 31% showed some evidence of depression or anxiety, compared with 24% of all adults.

## Which way do you lean?

Being in the middle often means translating. Your parents grew up with one set of rules about work, money, manners and family. Your children are writing a new set. You understand both, which is exactly why both sides expect you to back them.
