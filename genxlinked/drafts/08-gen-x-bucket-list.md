Somewhere between our first passport and our first reading glasses, a lot of us started keeping a list. Not always written down. Just a running sense of the places we'd regret not seeing.

We wanted to compare those lists across three of our countries: the USA, the UK and the Philippines. First, a confession about the data. We could not find a published survey that asks Gen X alone, in any of the three, where they most want to go. So we used the closest thing each country has, and we'll tell you exactly who was asked.

## United States: Hawaii, then Australia and Italy

AARP's travel research gives the best generational detail. In a survey [fielded in December 2016](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/consumer-media/articles/more-than-half-of-baby-boomers-have-a-bucket-list-aarp-says), slightly more than 1,600 American adults who had taken a leisure trip in the past two years were asked about their travel plans. Gen X were then aged roughly 36 to 51.

According to [TIME, reporting on AARP's research](https://time.com/4726565/bucket-list-travel-aarp-hawaii/), 58% of Gen Xers had a bucket list, and 76% of those lists included at least one trip. Gen X travel lists held an average of 12 destinations.

The survey's published destination rankings are for the generation just ahead of us, the baby boomers. Their top domestic dream was Hawaii (18%), then Alaska (12%), then California and Arizona (8% each). Abroad, it was Australia (13%), Italy (12%), Ireland or the UK (11%) and France (10%).

A newer survey asked Gen X directly but didn't publish a breakdown by generation. In June 2025, Talker Research, for the travel company Scenic Group, polled [2,000 Americans, 500 from each generation](https://talkerresearch.com/influencer-content-drives-travel-dysmorphia-in-younger-americans/) including Gen X. Across everyone, the top dream destinations were Japan and Paris (24% each), then Greece (19%), Australia (17%), Puerto Rico (16%) and Germany (14%). The one Gen X-specific finding: 22% of Gen X said they were looking toward historical and cultural tours.

## United Kingdom: the Northern Lights, by a distance

British surveys of the over-50s keep producing the same winner.

In 2021, Royal London asked [1,000 UK adults aged 55 and over](https://www.royallondon.com/media/press-releases/press-releases-2021/april/life-begins-at-fifty-five-new-study-reveals-how-the-over-55s-are-planning-to-live-life-to-the-fullest-in-a-post-pandemic-world/) what was on their bucket list. Seeing the Northern Lights came first, at 53%. Then came a trip on the Orient Express (42%), visiting one of the Seven Wonders of the World (36%), living or buying a house abroad (25%) and going on safari (22%).

Four years later, the result held. A OnePoll survey for eye-care provider Newmedica, published in October 2025, asked [2,000 adults aged 50 and over](https://www.newmedica.co.uk/news/2025/10/the-top-bucket-list-items-for-the-over-50s/). The Northern Lights were again number one and the Orient Express number two. Next came a cruise, seeing the cherry blossoms in Japan, reconnecting with old friends or family, an African safari and the Grand Canyon. That survey published the ranking but not percentages.

Neither survey splits out people in their fifties, so the oldest Gen Xers are in these results alongside people in their seventies and eighties.

## The Philippines: Japan, first and planned

For the Philippines we found no bucket-list survey at all, so this is the nearest thing: what people say they plan to do next.

Visa's Green Shoots Radar study, carried out in January 2024, surveyed [500 Filipino consumers aged 18 to 65](https://visa.com.ph/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/japan-ranks-as-top-leisure-travel-destination-for-filipino-travellers-visa-study.html). For leisure trips in the next 12 months, Japan led with 38%, followed by South Korea (29%) and Singapore (16%). Japan was also the place most had actually been in the past year (32%), ahead of Singapore (22%), Hong Kong (21%) and Thailand (18%).

That is a plan, not a lifelong dream, and the age range is far wider than Gen X. But it is the clearest signal we have.

## What the three lists share

Line the three countries up and one name keeps appearing: Japan. It tied for first among Americans of all ages in 2025, its cherry blossoms made the British over-50s' top five, and it tops Filipino travel plans.

The differences say something too. In the AARP data, American boomers' dreams started at home, with Hawaii and Alaska. British over-50s lean toward experiences more than places: a light show in the sky, a famous train. Filipino plans stay within Asia.

None of these surveys is a perfect fit for people born between 1965 and 1980. If a researcher out there wants to ask our generation directly, in all eight of our countries, we'd love to read the results.
