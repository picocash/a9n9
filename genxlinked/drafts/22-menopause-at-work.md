A post in an online Gen X women's forum shared a famous singer's one-liner about perimenopause, and thousands of women passed it on. We tried to trace the quote to an interview or a book and couldn't, so we won't repeat it here.

The numbers are easier to trace. "A full 13% of the women we surveyed experienced an adverse work outcome related to menopause symptoms, and about 11% were missing days of work because of these symptoms," said Dr Stephanie Faubion, director of Mayo Clinic Women's Health, when [her team's study](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-study-puts-price-tag-on-cost-of-menopause-symptoms-for-women-in-the-workplace) came out in April 2023.

## The United States: a price tag

Mayo Clinic surveyed 4,440 employed women aged 45 to 60, all its own primary-care patients, between March and June 2021. Their average age was 53.9. Scaled up to American women of that age, the researchers estimated $1.8 billion a year in lost work time, and $26.6 billion once medical costs were added.

The senior author, Dr Ekta Kapoor, pointed at the silence around it: "Women often fear bias, discrimination and stigmatization, and therefore may be reluctant to disclose their menopause symptoms to their workplace managers and others." One limit: 93% of the women were white, and most were married and college-educated.

## The UK: the biggest surveys

The CIPD, the professional body for HR, had YouGov survey 2,185 working women aged 40 to 60 in May and June 2023. Of those who had had symptoms, [two-thirds (67%)](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/reports/menopause-workplace-experiences/) said the effect on them at work was mostly negative. Most often that meant finding it harder to concentrate (79%) and more stress (68%). Over a quarter (27%) said menopause had hurt their career progression.

Support was thin. Only 24% said their organisation had a menopause policy or other support in place; 43% said it didn't, and about a third didn't know.

A larger survey in 2022, of about 4,000 women aged 45 to 55 for the Fawcett Society and Channel 4, put it more starkly. [One in 10](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/menopause-1-in-10-women-leave-job) had left work because of symptoms, 14% had cut their hours and 8% hadn't applied for a promotion.

## Australia: a smaller number, on purpose

Jean Hailes for Women's Health, which runs the National Women's Health Survey with Australian Government funding, found a lower figure in 2023: [7% of women aged 45 to 64](https://jeanhailes.org.au/research/womens-health-survey/menopause-in-australian-women) had missed work because of menopause symptoms. That is lower than many headlines suggest, but its chief executive, Sarah White, did not wave it away: "We must address workplace flexibility for women with severe symptoms."

## Singapore and the region

In Asia, the data are newer. The National University of Singapore's medical school and HeyVenus Integrated Healthscience, a company led by chief executive Christina Ang, surveyed 1,741 working women in Singapore, Vietnam, Australia, Japan and Indonesia, and [published in April 2025](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1080891). About half said symptoms significantly disrupted their work and daily lives, and 61% said stigma kept them from talking about it. The study found that only about a third of companies in the region had a menopause-friendly policy.

We found no national workplace survey for the Philippines.
