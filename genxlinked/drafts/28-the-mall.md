Two posts in an online Gen X forum said the same thing in different ways: one person just wanted to go to the mall again, and another said the stuff we grew up with is simply gone.

The writer Alexandra Lange has a name for what we were really doing there. The mall atrium, she writes in *Meet Me by the Fountain*, was "the most important interior space of the mall for the adolescent". The questions that got us there were simple: "Is my friend hanging out at the mall? Is my crush?"

## America: fewer malls, fewer hours

The United States had about 1,500 malls in 2005 and around 1,150 by 2022, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond's history of the mall. That count had held steady for three years. "If the pandemic didn't cause a dramatic decline in the number of these centers," Tom McGee of the International Council of Shopping Centers told the Richmond Fed, "then almost nothing will."

The drift away started earlier than most of us remember. The Richmond Fed notes that the average time consumers spent in a mall each month fell sharply during the decade older Gen X were teenagers.

::: stat
4 hours | a month Americans spent in malls in 1990, down from 12 in 1980 | Richmond Fed, 2022
:::

So the mall we miss was already emptying while we were in it. We just didn't notice, because we were by the fountain.

## Britain: the empty units

In the UK, the loss shows up as gaps rather than demolitions. Knight Frank, the property consultancy, put the shopping centre vacancy rate at 16.5% in the third quarter of 2025, slightly better than 16.9% the quarter before. Retail parks, the out-of-town sheds with car parks, were at 6.1%, according to the Local Data Company.

## Singapore: the mall as the town square

Singapore had its own mall generation. In the early to mid-1980s, groups of teenagers gathered at Far East Plaza and then at The Centrepoint on Orchard Road, and the newspapers called them the "Centrepoint Kids". A survey of them in December 1985 interviewed young people aged 10 to 23, according to an encyclopedia summary of Straits Times reports from the time.

Four decades on, Singapore's shops are fuller than Britain's. The Urban Redevelopment Authority reported island-wide retail vacancy of 6.9% at the end of September 2025, down from 7.1% the quarter before.

## The Philippines: still where everyone goes

In the Philippines, the mall never stopped being where people meet. SM Supermalls runs 89 malls nationwide and counted 1.4 billion visits in 2025, an average of 115 million a month, according to figures reported in February 2026. On weekends over the Christmas peak, daily visits averaged 5.5 million.

That is a company's own count, so read it as a measure of scale rather than an independent audit. It is still an enormous number of people choosing the same air-conditioned place on a Saturday.

Metro Manila malls are filling up again too. Colliers Philippines put retail space vacancy there at 11.4% at the end of September 2025, close to the 9.3% recorded before the pandemic in the third quarter of 2019.

One caveat: these vacancy rates come from different firms measuring different kinds of space, so they show direction, not a ranking.

The US numbers we found count malls, not visitors, and we could not find a comparable national count of mall visits for the UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland or New Zealand. If your country publishes one, tell us.

Lange's question still works, though the answer depends on the map. In Manila, your friend probably is at the mall: SM alone recorded an average of 115 million visits a month in 2025.
