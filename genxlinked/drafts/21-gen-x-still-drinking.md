A post in an online Gen X forum shared a headline saying most Americans were drinking less, except one age group, and the comments filled up quickly with people checking which group they were in.

The headline came from real data. In August 2023, researchers behind the long-running Monitoring the Future study reported that binge drinking among American adults aged 35 to 50 had hit a record. Megan Patrick, the study's principal investigator at the University of Michigan, said that ["midlife adults ages 35 to 50 have the highest level of binge drinking we have ever seen in that age group."](https://newschannel9.com/news/nation-world/study-shows-rise-in-binge-drinking-drug-use-marijuana-cannabis-alcohol-vaping-cigarettes-opioids-hallucinogens-substance-abuse)

In 2022, that band covered people born roughly 1972 to 1987: younger Gen X and older millennials.

## The record, and what came after

The study counts binge drinking as having five or more drinks in the past two weeks. Among 35-to-50-year-olds, it rose from 23% in 2012 to [29% in 2022](https://nida.nih.gov/node/51258). In 2023 it eased to 27%, still up on five and ten years earlier, while among 19-to-30-year-olds binge drinking fell to an [all-time low](https://src.isr.umich.edu/?p=1114). Daily drinking among the 35-to-50s also fell, to 8% in 2023, the lowest the study has recorded.

## Gallup's version: the middle held out, then folded

Gallup asks a simpler question every July: do you drink at all? In 2025, [54% of Americans said yes](https://news.gallup.com/poll/693362/drinking-rate-new-low-alcohol-concerns-surge.aspx), down from 62% in 2023. Gallup's age bands are 18 to 34, 35 to 54 and 55 and older, so none of them is Gen X exactly.

The middle band is where the thread's headline came from. Between 2023 and 2024, drinking fell among 18-to-34s (58% to 50%) and among those 55 and older (61% to 54%). Among 35-to-54s it went up, from 66% to 70%. Then in 2025 it dropped to 56%, level with the over-55s. Young adults stayed at 50%.

::: stat
70% | of Americans aged 35–54 said they drank in 2024, while other age groups fell | Gallup, 2024
:::

Opinion moved too. Half (50%) of 35-to-54s now say moderate drinking is bad for your health, up from 39% in 2023. Among those 55 and older it rose from 29% to 48%.

## Elsewhere, midlife is near the top

**England.** In the Health Survey for England 2022, [24% of adults](https://nhsd-proxy.openprescribing.net/data-and-information/publications/statistical/health-survey-for-england/2022-part-1/adult-drinking) drank over 14 units a week. Those aged 55 to 64 were the most likely, 38% of men and 23% of women. UK guidelines say men and women should not normally drink more than 14 units a week.

**Australia.** In 2022–23, about one in three people in their 50s (32%) and 60s (33%) drank at risky levels, according to the [Australian Institute of Health and Welfare](https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/alcohol/alcohol-tobacco-other-drugs-australia/contents/priority-populations/older-people). That was lower than 18-to-24s (42%) and has been fairly stable since 2007.

**Canada.** In 2023, [54% of Canadian adults](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/app/7085-snapshot-alcohol-consumption-levels-canada-half-canadian-adults-reported-not-drinking-any) had no alcohol in the past week. Among 18-to-22-year-olds, 8% had seven or more drinks that week; in every older age group it was between 14% and 17%. Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health, from 2023, sets out risk zones by drinks per week.

**Ireland.** The Healthy Ireland Survey 2025 found 26% of people [binge drinking](https://www.irishtimes.com/health/2025/11/07/almost-two-in-five-younger-women-drink-to-harmful-levels-health-survey-reveals/), down from 30% in 2015. The worry there is the young: 39% of women aged 15 to 24 drank at a harmful level.

**New Zealand.** Ministry of Health data show [hazardous drinking](https://figure.nz/chart/LJu9XAwSqV5RqBhh-MdWlTmljcoNIvDhz) among men fell from 28.9% in the year to June 2020 to 21.9% in the year to June 2025, and among women from 13.8% to 11.5%.

One caveat: each country measures risky drinking differently, so these are not a league table. We found no comparable midlife figures for Singapore or the Philippines.

The most striking line is still the American one. In 2024, the 35-to-54s were the only age group in Gallup's poll where the share who drink went up. A year later they had joined everyone else.
