A post in an online Gen X forum about the night before a first colonoscopy turned into a long, cheerful pile of prep stories: the drink, the flavour, the evening spent close to the bathroom.

Nobody in that thread was the first to joke about it. Back in 2003 the humorist Dave Barry wrote about visiting a giant model of a human colon on a 20-city tour, and admitted that instead of asking his doctor about screening he asked Gene Weingarten what the procedure was like. Weingarten said it "was not nearly as bad as I imagined."

What surprised us, reading the thread, was how many different ages people gave for their first one. That is partly personal history. It is also because countries start their public screening at very different ages, and most of them don't start with a colonoscopy at all.

## Mostly a kit in the post

In most of the places we checked, the public programme sends a home stool test called FIT, the faecal immunochemical test. For many of us, in other words, screening starts with an envelope, not a jug of prep.

**England** offers the home FIT kit to people aged 50 to 74 every two years. The NHS page says your first kit arrives at 50, 52 or 54, depending on when you turned 50. **Scotland** posts the same kind of test every two years to everyone aged 50 to 74. **Wales** has invited people aged 50 to 74 since 9 October 2024.

**Northern Ireland** currently screens people aged 59 to 74 every two years, and plans to extend that gradually to everyone aged 50 to 74 by 2031.

**Ireland's** BowelScreen widened on 1 April 2026 to everyone aged 57 to 71, invited every two years for a home FIT kit. "Just one screening test in your lifetime can reduce the likelihood of developing bowel cancer," said Jennifer Carroll MacNeill, the Minister for Health, at the launch.

**Australia** lowered its starting age in July 2024. People aged 45 to 49 can ask for their first free kit; from 50 to 74, a kit arrives in the mail every two years.

**New Zealand's** free programme sends a home test every two years from 58. Health New Zealand says the starting age is dropping to 56, first in eight districts from 30 September 2026, with the rest of the country expected to follow in the first half of 2027.

**Canada** runs screening province by province. In British Columbia, BC Cancer's guidance for most people aged 50 to 74 is a FIT every two years.

## The colonoscopy-first country

**The United States** entry in our chart is different in kind: a recommendation, not an invitation programme. The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening for adults aged 45 to 75, and lists several options, from a yearly stool test to a colonoscopy every ten years.

**Singapore's** Screen for Life programme offers a FIT for citizens and permanent residents aged 50 and above, available again one year after the last one.

**The Philippines** is the gap in our list. We could not confirm a national bowel screening programme from an official page we could open; if you are a reader in Manila or Cebu and know how it works there, write to us.

This is not medical advice; ask your doctor about screening.

The spread is wider than any of us would guess from our own group chat: an Australian can ask for a free kit at 45, while a New Zealander waits until 58 in most of the country.
