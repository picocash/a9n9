In 1984 the oldest of Gen X turned 19 and the youngest turned four. If you were a teenager that year, the world you lived in depended a lot on which city you woke up in.

Dublin and Singapore had little in common in 1984 beyond a fondness for the radio. Here is what that year looked like in each, using only what the records show.

## The soundtrack

**Dublin.** The Irish singles chart in 1984 reads like a mixtape. The year opened with The Flying Pickets' ["Only You"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_number-one_singles_of_1984_(Ireland)) at number one. Then came Queen's "Radio Ga Ga", Cyndi Lauper's "Girls Just Want to Have Fun", Nena's "99 Red Balloons", Lionel Richie's "Hello", Duran Duran's "The Reflex", Wham!'s "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go", George Michael's "Careless Whisper" and Stevie Wonder's "I Just Called to Say I Love You".

Frankie Goes to Hollywood's "Two Tribes" had the most weeks at number one that year, seven in all. Wham! had the most number ones, with two. And the year closed, in the week ending 29 December, with Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas?".

The big outdoor gig of the summer was on 8 July, when the [Slane Concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Ireland) was headlined by Bob Dylan, with Santana and UB40 on the bill and Van Morrison and Bono as guests.

**Singapore.** The big local music story was homegrown. Xinyao, a Mandarin song style [started in the early 1980s by students from secondary schools, junior colleges and polytechnics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinyao), was built around friendship, school life and teenage romance. In 1984 the first xinyao album, *Tomorrow We'll be 21*, sold about 30,000 copies, a record at the time.

Xinyao was, in the genre's own shorthand, "songs created by young people in Singapore": music by teenagers, for teenagers.

## On the telly

**Singapore.** Television in 1984 belonged to *The Awakening*. The Mandarin drama ran on SBC 8 from [6 February to 12 October 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Awakening_(TV_series)), with Huang Wenyong and Xiang Yun in the lead roles, and its first series reached an audience of 800,000 adults. The National Library Board's BiblioAsia calls it ["a hugely popular Mandarin drama series"](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/vol-20/issue-3/oct-dec-2024/colour-tv-history-singapore/) about the trials of Singapore's early Chinese immigrants.

**Dublin.** In Ireland, 1984 was the year RTÉ Radio first broadcast [*Morning Ireland*, on 5 November](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Ireland). Three days later, RTÉ Television's first newsreader, Charles Mitchel, read his final bulletin.

Pirate radio was the other big draw. In September 1984, Radio Nova ran a phone-in giveaway worth [five grand](https://www.thejournal.ie/radio-phone-in-nova-1691310-Oct2014/). Listeners had to call after the station played three particular songs in a row. When they did, so many people rang that "the city's fragile phone system simply collapsed under the demand" for a few hours.

## Getting around

Both cities were building trains.

**Dublin.** On 23 July 1984, the [DART began running between Bray and Howth](https://www.rte.ie/archives/2014/0723/632532-dart-from-bray-to-howth). Later that year, on 19 October, the last passenger ferry across the River Liffey [made its final run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Ireland).

**Singapore.** The MRT was still a building site. Construction had begun with a ground-breaking at Shan Road on 22 October 1983, and in [January 1984 work began on the Outram Park to Clementi stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Mass_Rapid_Transit_(Singapore)), followed by Clementi to Lakeside in July. Passengers would not ride it until 7 November 1987. In the air, Changi Airport opened its [second runway on 17 April 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Singapore).

## Big moments

**Dublin.** On 12 August 1984, John Treacy of Villierstown, County Waterford, [won silver in the marathon](https://www.rte.ie/archives/2014/0812/636602-john-treacy-wins-olympic-silver). A month earlier, on 19 July, an earthquake centred on the Llŷn Peninsula in Wales [was felt in Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Ireland).

**Singapore.** From 1 to 16 December, Singapore hosted the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_AFC_Asian_Cup) for the first time, with every match at the National Stadium in Kallang. A crowd of 55,000 watched Singapore beat India 2–0 on 2 December. Saudi Arabia won the final 2–0 against China.

Earlier in the year, Parkway Parade [opened on 9 March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_in_Singapore), and on 31 October Creative Technology launched the Cubic 99, a computer made in Singapore. In November, the government started an "Eat Frozen Pork" campaign as pig farms were phased out; by Wikipedia's account, it did not achieve substantial results.

## What things cost

**Dublin.** The Central Statistics Office publishes national average prices, and its nearest figures are for 1983, the year before. Then, a pint of draught stout cost on average [108p, a loaf of bread 42p and a litre of petrol 60p](https://www.cso.ie/en/media/csoie/releasespublications/documents/otherreleases/thatwasthenthisisnow.pdf). The CSO also notes that the years 1973 to 1988 were a time of high inflation in Ireland, and that price levels more than quadrupled between 1973 and 1983.

**Singapore.** We couldn't find a reliable published list of everyday Singapore prices for 1984, so we are not going to guess. If you remember what a bowl of noodles or a bus ride cost that year, we would like to hear it.

## Two cities, one generation

Put side by side, 1984 shows two cities building for the future in very different ways. Dublin teenagers had the DART, pirate radio and an Olympic medal to cheer. Singapore teenagers had *The Awakening*, xinyao and an MRT taking shape under their feet.
