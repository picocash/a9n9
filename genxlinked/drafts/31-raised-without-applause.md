Somewhere in the 23,002 comments under "The Psychology Of Gen X (Raised Without Applause)", someone says we don't fear being alone because we always were. Twelve thousand people liked that. The video, from a channel called Big Brain Explains, has passed 6 million views since February.

It tells a story we already half believe: latchkey kids, divorced parents, nobody clapping, so we grew up self-reliant and quiet. Nancy Tippins, who chaired a 2020 National Academies committee on generations at work, warned that this kind of generational research ["has serious limitations"](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2020/07/categorizing-workers-needs-by-generation-such-as-baby-boomers-or-millennials-is-not-supported-by-research-or-useful-for-workforce-management). So we checked the video's specific claims.

## What it gets right

The feeling. The comment section is the real story: people remembering reading the encyclopedia when bored, taping songs off the radio, minding younger siblings as the oldest kid, and learning never to tell a father you were bored. One commenter, a former latchkey kid, wrote that "people call it neglect now, but it was training" (928 likes). Another noted that only Gen Xers would watch a video about Gen X, which 8,300 people agreed was the most Gen X thing ever.

YouTube labels the video "Made with AI": sounds or visuals altered or fully generated. That isn't a charge. GenXLinked is AI-assisted too, and says so at the bottom of every piece. The difference that matters is sources.

## Seven claims, checked

**"By 1984, roughly 7 million kids between ages 5 and 13 were regularly unsupervised after school."** Close to a real number, attached to the wrong thing. The Census Bureau counted 28.9 million schoolchildren aged 5 to 13 in December 1984, and [7.1 million spent some time after school without a parent](https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/1987/demographics/p23-149.pdf). Most were with another adult; about 2.1 million cared for themselves or were minded by another child. The bigger number had been in circulation: in May 1984 *Education Week* reported the Children's Defense Fund's estimate of [7 million children 13 and under](https://www.edweek.org/education/schools-urged-to-seek-solutions-to-troubles-of-latchkey-children/1984/05) without adult supervision, and the Census report notes estimates presented to Congress ranging from 2 to 15 million.

**"Divorce rates exploded throughout the 70s and 80s."** Half right. The US rate per 1,000 people went from 2.5 in 1965 to 3.5 in 1970 and 4.8 in 1975, peaked at 5.3 in 1979 and again in 1981, then [eased to 5.0 by 1985 and 4.8 in 1987](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/mvsr/supp/mv38_12s2.pdf). The explosion was the 1970s. The 1980s were a plateau.

**"The people who built the internet."** Not quite. Pew defines Gen X as [born 1965 to 1980](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/01/17/where-millennials-end-and-generation-z-begins/). Vint Cerf (born 1943) and Bob Kahn (1938) designed the internet's core protocols, and Tim Berners-Lee (1955) invented the web. Gen X built much of what ran on it: Marc Andreessen (1971) co-wrote the Mosaic browser, Larry Page and Sergey Brin (both 1973) founded Google, Jerry Yang (1968) Yahoo and Pierre Omidyar (1967) eBay.

**"Barely on social media."** The opposite. In Pew's 2026 survey, [71% of Americans aged 50 to 64 use Facebook](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/), against 69% of 18 to 29 year olds; 78% of 30 to 49 year olds do. And 85% of 50 to 64 year olds use YouTube.

**"Psychologists call this a high contingency environment."** The video cites B. F. Skinner's *Science and Human Behavior* (1953). Contingency is Skinner's word: it appears more than 150 times in the book. The phrase "high contingency environment" does not appear in it at all.

**"Research shows Gen Xers report significantly lower rates of seeking social support during stress."** The video lists Sheldon and Antony (2019), *Journal of Adult Development* 26(2), pages 163–171. We couldn't find it. Crossref's record of that volume lists no such paper, and pages 161 to 171 belong to a study of Nigerian young adults in issue 3. The nearest title we found, a 2013 paper on generational differences in social media use at work, [was about distraction](https://docs.rwu.edu/gsb_fp/13), not stress.

**"When information costs you something to obtain, your brain holds it more permanently."** Wrong citation, half-right idea. Craik and Lockhart's 1972 paper says memory lasts longer the [more deeply you process meaning](https://psych205.stanford.edu/papers/CraikLockhart72.pdf), and the authors call for separating "amount of effort from depth as such". The closer idea is Robert Bjork's "desirable difficulties", such as spacing study and testing yourself, and the Bjorks stress that ["many difficulties are undesirable"](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf).

One timing detail: "the youngest came of age right as 2008 nuked the economy." By Pew's definition, the youngest Gen Xers turned 28 in 2008.

## Why it still feels true

In a classroom test published in 1949, the psychologist Bertram Forer gave his students a personality sketch he said was based on their test answers. It was [taken from a newsstand astrology column](https://skepdic.com/forer.html), and the class rated its accuracy at 4.26 out of 5. Descriptions broad enough to fit everyone feel personal. Pew now [reports on generations far less often](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/05/22/how-pew-research-center-will-report-on-generations-moving-forward/), noting that standard surveys can compare age groups but not follow them over time.

The video is American: Watergate, Iran-Contra, duck and cover. Elsewhere the divorce story ran differently. Ireland's constitution said "no law shall be enacted providing for the grant of a dissolution of marriage". In 1986 Irish voters [kept that ban, 935,843 to 538,279](https://www.referendum.ie/archive/referendum-on-dissolution-of-marriage-tenth-amendment-of-the-constitution-bill-1986/), and voted to lift it only in November 1995 [by 818,842 votes to 809,728](https://www.referendum.ie/archive/referendum-on-dissolution-of-marriage-fifteenth-amendment-of-the-constitution-no-2-bill-1995/).

In the Philippines, the wait goes on. It is the only country other than the Vatican where a married couple [cannot legally end their marriage](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/7/5/we-are-not-criminals-philippines-considers-making-divorce-legal), apart from annulment and limited divorce for Filipino Muslims. The House passed a divorce bill in May 2024; as of September 2025, eight new bills [were still waiting in committee](https://verafiles.org/articles/fact-check-reel-on-divorce-bill-approval-in-ph-needs-context).
