"It's the exact same messaging that we'd given out ... 30 years ago," Suzanne Bernier, an instructor with the Disaster Recovery Institute of Canada, told CP24 in November 2026. She was talking about the advice to be ready to manage on your own for three days.

She has a point. The number three turns up almost everywhere.

## Three days, give or take

In the UK, the Cabinet Office launched its Be Ready campaign on 22 October 2026, asking households in England and Wales to "be prepared for three days of disruption". The checklist runs to bottled water, tins, a torch, a battery or wind-up radio, a power bank and cash, in case card payments fail.

Public Safety Canada asks people to "be prepared to go at least 72 hours without essential services." In New Zealand, "households should have enough water for three days," Sarah Drake of Stats NZ said in 2022, which she put at 9 litres a person. In the Philippines, the Office of Civil Defense's go bag includes ready-to-eat food and bottled water "that can last for three days", and should be checked every three months.

The US is vaguer. Ready.gov asks for "one gallon per person per day for several days".

Singapore went for a rehearsal. Exercise SG Ready, from 1 to 15 February 2026, simulated digital disruption: at 3pm on the first day, several apps went into an hour of pretend maintenance. People were told to try FM radio if digital channels went down.

## How many of us actually do it

Not many. A Cabinet Office survey this year found just 13% of UK households said they were largely or totally prepared, Al Jazeera reported. In New Zealand's 2021 General Social Survey, 83% of households had enough food for three days but only 47% enough water. Statistics Canada found 47% of Canadians had an emergency supply kit in 2014, and 43% had stored water or another source.

FEMA's 2022 survey of 7,145 Americans found 33% had assembled or updated supplies in the past year. In the Philippines, a 2017 Harvard Humanitarian Initiative survey of 4,368 households found 82% had no go bag. Money was the main reason people gave, at 47.5%, and only 36% of respondents saw themselves as fully ready.
