A post in an online Gen X forum asked why it is always her job to remember everything. In the replies, someone brought up a newer word for one corner of it: mankeeping.

The word was coined by Angelica Puzio Ferrara, a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford. ["This is the labor that women take on to shore up losses in men's social networks,"](https://gender.stanford.edu/news/structural-burden-mens-declining-social-networks) she said in a 2024 profile by Stanford's Clayman Institute for Gender Research. "In the US, about one in five men claim they have no close friends."

## The remembering is work

The idea that thinking about the house is a job has a research base. Sociologist Allison Daminger interviewed 70 people in 35 couples for a study published in 2019. She split the "cognitive labour" of a household into four parts: anticipating what's needed, finding the options, deciding, and checking it got done.

Women did more of it in [26 of the 32 heterosexual couples](https://daily.jstor.org/the-gendered-labor-of-noticing-and-anticipating). They did far more of the anticipating and the checking. Men took a bigger part in the deciding, often after the women had done the research.

::: stat
4.6 | of nine areas of home life where women led the thinking, on average (men: 1.6) | Daminger, 2019
:::

"Men did not opt out of cognitive labor altogether," Daminger wrote, "rather, they participated in ways that tended to maximize the ratio of power exerted to labor completed." On average, couples shared 2.2 of the nine areas. Men led most often on maintenance or money.

## The hours are measured too

The thinking is hard to count. The doing isn't, and national time-use surveys show the same tilt everywhere we looked.

In the UK, women spent [3 hours 32 minutes a day on unpaid work in March 2024](https://natcen.ac.uk/TimeUseStudy), 57 minutes more than men's 2 hours 35 minutes. In Australia, [women averaged 4 hours 53 minutes in 2024](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/7-ways-we-spent-our-time-2024) and men 3 hours 52 minutes. In Canada's 2022 survey, [women averaged 3.7 hours a day and men 2.6](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/hub-carrefour/quality-life-qualite-vie/society-societe/time-use-emploi-temp-eng.htm).

In the US, the 2024 American Time Use Survey found [87% of women and 74% of men did some housework or other household activity](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06262025.htm) on an average day. Men had more leisure: 5.5 hours, against 4.7 for women.

## Singapore's version: who thinks who does it

We found no official time-use figures for Singapore, so the best numbers there are about perception. Ipsos, working with the non-profit United Women Singapore, asked 500 married Singaporeans in November 2020. [43% of women said they were mainly responsible for house cleaning](https://www.ipsos.com/en-sg/singapore-women-are-doing-more-unpaid-domestic-care-work-men-think-they-are); only 24% of men said their wives were.

The gap held elsewhere: 52% against 32% for laundry, 59% against 45% for cooking. And 54% of women were happy with the household and caring arrangements, against 75% of men.

## Mankeeping gets a scale

Ferrara and her Stanford colleague Dylan Vergara set out the idea in a [2024 paper in *Psychology of Men & Masculinities*](https://doi.org/10.1037/men0000494). Their argument: as men's friendships thin out, female partners pick up the social and emotional work those friends used to do. One New York therapist, Justin Lioi, told *The New York Times* in 2025 that partners of his male clients had become their ["unofficial therapists."](https://thenightly.com.au/lifestyle/the-new-york-times-why-women-are-weary-of-the-emotional-labour-of-mankeeping-c-19511606)

In 2026 a team including both of them built a questionnaire to measure it, testing it on 402 women in the US and 395 in the UK. In the journal *Sex Roles*, they reported that [five of its six dimensions were linked to women's self-reported depression, anxiety and stress](https://www.psypost.org/mankeeping-takes-a-toll-on-womens-mental-health-and-relationship-satisfaction-study-suggests/). "'Mankeeping' is something many women express that they have been doing for their partners for quite some time," said Vincent Mancini, the lead author.

The research is new and the authors call their results preliminary. The time-use numbers are not. In Australia, Canada and the UK, women did roughly an hour more unpaid work every day, which by our arithmetic comes to around 30 hours a month.
