A post in an online Gen X forum asked what we got up to alone after school, and the replies read like a confession: a key on a string, tinned ravioli, and parents who, one poster reckoned, would be serving life sentences by today's standards.

In 2008 Lenore Skenazy, a columnist for *The New York Sun*, let her nine-year-old son make his own way home from a department store by subway and bus, with a map, a transit pass and $20 but no phone. "In my day," she wrote, "I doubt that would have struck anyone as particularly brave."

## The latchkey count

Her day was well documented. In June 1983 a US Senate Children's Caucus held a policy forum on "latchkey children", and researchers put the number unsupervised after school anywhere from 2 to 15 million.

The Census Bureau then asked. In December 1984, 25% of the 28.9 million American schoolchildren aged 5 to 13 spent some time after school without a parent, and about 2.1 million of them were caring for themselves or being minded by another child. It was usually brief: about 3 in 10 of the children in self-care were on their own for less than an hour, and only 1 in 10 for three hours or more.

Age mattered most. The share with no adult at all after school rose from 1% of 5-year-olds to 14% of 13-year-olds. Among 12- and 13-year-olds whose mothers worked full time, 22% were in self-care.

## Sixteen countries, one key

A generation later, the Policy Studies Institute in London coordinated surveys of 18,303 children aged 7 to 15, and many of their parents, in 16 countries between 2010 and 2012. Finland came first by a distance. A majority of Finnish children could go to places within walking distance alone at age 7. England was 7th. Ireland was 12th and Australia 13th, ahead of only Portugal, Italy and South Africa.

Weighted across all 16 countries and every age, parents said 67% of children could cross main roads alone and 65% could travel home from school alone. Going out after dark was the freedom most often held back: 22%.

In Ireland, half of 15-year-olds surveyed did not travel home from school alone, according to their parents. Only in Finland, Sweden, Japan and Denmark were a majority of children of any age allowed out after dark. The authors flag that their samples may not represent each country fully.

The study did not cover the Philippines or Singapore, and we found no comparable count for either.
