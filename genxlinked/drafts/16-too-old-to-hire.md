A post in an online Gen X forum summed it up in one line: 57, too old to be hired, too young to retire. One of the replies came from someone laid off from a tech job at 58.

Marina Drysdale knows the feeling. At 61, she had been looking for a lasting job in North Carolina since moving there in February 2022. ["I'm not decrepit,"](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2025/01/20/older-jobseekers-face-age-discrimination-negative-health-impacts/) she told North Carolina Health News in January 2025. "I'm very active, and I'm not planning to retire anytime soon — if I get a job."

## First, most people this age are working

In the six English-speaking countries we cover, the picture at 55 to 64 is not one of mass exclusion. In 2024, by OECD figures, about two in three people that age had a job in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia, more in Ireland and New Zealand.

The trouble starts when you lose the job you have.

## Then the search takes longer

In the US, the Bureau of Labor Statistics counts how long unemployed people have been looking. In 2024, the [median spell for jobseekers aged 55 to 64 was 12.0 weeks](https://www.bls.gov/cps/data/aa2024/cpsaat31.htm), against 9.6 weeks for all unemployed people and 10.8 weeks for those 45 to 54.

The long tail is worse. By our calculation from the same table, 26.3% of unemployed 55 to 64 year olds had been looking for 27 weeks or more, compared with 21.4% of all the unemployed.

North Carolina's Department of Commerce tracked people laid off in 2017. One quarter later, 61% of jobseekers aged 55 and over were back in work, against 68% of those aged 25 to 54 and 74% of those under 25, North Carolina Health News reported.

## The UK gap is smaller than you'd think

In Britain, the Department for Work and Pensions publishes a yearly check on the over-50s. In the period to April–June 2025, [29.5% of unemployed 50 to 64 year olds had been out of work for a year or more](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/economic-labour-market-status-of-individuals-aged-50-and-over-trends-over-time-september-2025/economic-labour-market-status-of-individuals-aged-50-and-over-trends-over-time-september-2025), against 27.9% of 35 to 49 year olds.

The department says that 1.6-point gap is not statistically significant. A year earlier it was 4.4 points. Meanwhile 71.6% of UK 50 to 64 year olds were in work in 2025.

## Is it the employer, or the market?

Unemployment numbers can't tell you why. A field experiment can. Economists David Neumark, Ian Burn and Patrick Button sent more than 40,000 matched fake applications to over 13,000 US job ads in 12 cities, identical except for age: roughly 30, 50 or 65.

::: stat
47% | lower callback rate for older women applying for admin jobs than younger women | Neumark, Burn & Button, via San Francisco Fed, 2017
:::

Older women applying for administrative jobs [got callbacks 7.6% of the time, against 14.4% for younger women](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2017/02/age-discrimination-and-hiring-older-workers/). In retail sales the rates were 18.4% against 28.7% for women, and 14.70% against 20.89% for men. "Women experience more age discrimination than men do," the San Francisco Fed's summary concluded.

The caveat: the "older" applicants were 64 to 66, not 57, and the jobs were admin, retail, janitorial and security roles.

Workers notice. In a January 2025 survey by AARP, the US advocacy group for people over 50, three-quarters of over-50s thought their age would count against them if they looked for work, North Carolina Health News reported.

## Singapore moved the goalposts on purpose

Singapore sets the ages officially. Since 1 July 2026, [the minimum retirement age is 64 and the re-employment age is 69](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/re-employment). Those ages apply to employees born on or after 1 July 1963, which takes in everyone now in their early sixties and younger.

Most of us won't be offered that. In the US, a 55 to 64 year old who loses a job is, by the median, out of work for twelve weeks, and more than one in four unemployed people that age have been looking for over six months.
