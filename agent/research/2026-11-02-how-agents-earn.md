# How AI Agents Earn Beyond Token Speculation

## Executive Summary

- **Sell Work, Not an Agent Coin**: The strongest transferable examples are paid products, services and transaction fees. Felix's dashboard reports **$212,303.66 in lifetime Stripe revenue**, excluding crypto payments, but only **$1,756.40 over the displayed last 30 days**. A successful launch is not recurring income. -> Start with a buyer and a useful deliverable. [29]
- **Separate Wealth From Revenue**: Truth Terminal transferred tokens valued above **$22M** in a January 2025 OTC transaction; that valuation is not a disclosed cash sale price or customer revenue. -> Do not benchmark your business against token treasuries. [28]
- **Fees Can Be Real**: DeFiLlama records **$5.75M in Bankr fees and $2.3M in protocol revenue for January 2026**. -> Distinguish customer fees, creator allocations and the operator's retained revenue.
- **Paid-Agent Markets Remain Thin**: An October 2026 x402 tracker identifies **91 qualifying recipient wallets** and **$793K of filtered monthly volume**, versus 37,495 wallets receiving something. -> Seek repeat buyers, not transaction-count screenshots. [32]
- **Transparency Can Earn Small Payments**: Orrery reportedly earned a few hundred sats by auditing claims and precommitting report hashes to Nostr. -> Your ledger fits verification work, but receipts do not establish a large market. [19]
- **Gross Sales Are Not Profit**: Andon's cafe sold about **$9K** while spending **$38K** over its first approximately two months. -> Publish costs and cash flow alongside revenue. [18]
- **Followers Are Often the Wrong Gate**: X's revenue-sharing page requires **5M organic impressions in three months and 500 verified followers**, plus subscription and payout requirements. Direct research sales need neither threshold. -> Treat X as distribution, not your initial payer.
- **Recommended Order**: Over 90 days, prioritize **commissioned research, monitoring retainers, paid verification jobs, a niche paid brief, then a metered data API**. Affiliate links and tips should be extras, not the business plan.

Here, "non-token" means **not issuing your own tradable agent token**. Charging in USDC or receiving Bitcoin tips still qualifies. Historical figures below retain their original periods; missing revenue or same-period audiences are identified rather than estimated from token prices.

**Truth Terminal's decision was to monetize a cultural phenomenon, not to build a conventional service business.** The OTC sale was human-facilitated, while its original grant came before the requested period. For your agent, reproduce the useful distinction between free discovery content and paid depth, not the six-figure holding gate.

**Zerebro illustrates why creative output and financial outcomes need separate ledgers.** NFT sales and music releases are products; follower counts, streams and token capitalization are different metrics. Its founder scandal also shows why verifiable delivery history should attach to an accountable operator, not only to a personality. [25][22]

**Decision:** None of these cases demonstrates that a small, transparent account can reliably earn a living merely by posting. They demonstrate distribution, patronage and speculative demand, with only partial disclosure of realized business income.

## Felix/OpenClaw: Real Product Sales, With Important Qualifications

Felix is the clearest OpenClaw-style example here of an agent-associated business selling ordinary products. In Nat Eliason's Bankless interview, the first Felix Craft information product had generated **$41K**; ClawMart had brought in almost **$11K over the preceding month**, charging creators **$20/month plus 10% of sales**. These are interview-period figures, not current monthly recurring revenue. [3]

The business made a PDF about hiring an AI, built its website and connected payments. It was not entirely independent of human enablement: the first launch needed Nat to supply the appropriate Stripe API keys. Nor was it a clean zero-distribution experiment: Nat describes viral OpenClaw articles drawing hundreds of thousands of views and one post around **1M views**. The Mac Mini cost roughly **$600-$700**. [3]

| Required distinction | Evidence and implication |
|---|---|
| **Actual product receipts** | The dashboard updated October 26, 2026 reports **$212,303.66 lifetime Stripe revenue**, explicitly excluding crypto payments. It displays **$1,756.40 over 30 days**. These are sales receipts, not demonstrated net profit. [29] |
| **Token-related receipts** | A February 13 post reports **$62,013 total revenue since February 2**, excluding roughly $50K in token holdings. But the quoted breakdown includes **8.9 ETH, approximately $18,271, in FELIX trading fees that week**. Excluding holdings did **not** make that total non-token revenue. [23] |
| **Audience at the sales window** | No matched Felix follower/customer count is disclosed in these sources. The founder's viral reach is documented, but should not be substituted for paying customers. [3] |
| **What went wrong** | A hosted OpenClaw-service idea was abandoned after about a week because its product, margins and differentiation were insufficient. A working agent is not automatically a viable hosting business. [3] |
| **Own-token requirement** | The **$29 guide**, product marketplace and custom-service model can operate without a token. FELIX token holdings and trading fees are separate streams. [29][23] |

The dashboard is useful precisely because it permits a less glamorous conclusion than the launch headlines: substantial lifetime receipts can coexist with a much smaller recent sales window. Those measures do not establish why sales changed, but they rule out treating lifetime revenue as MRR. [29]

**Decision:** For your agent, the transferable product is an evidence pack, integration or reusable dataset. The transferable operating pattern is disclosed human payment setup plus separately reported sales, token receipts, costs and refunds. OpenClaw is infrastructure, not a revenue mechanism by itself.

**Decision:** A transparent agent should make the cash ledger harder to fool, not simply make more transactions. Selling low-overhead research avoids the inventory, staffing and custody risks exposed by these experiments.

[8] |
| **Olas Mech research/services market** | The official page reports **$110,436 total task payments/marketplace fees**, including legacy contracts, all time as of October 29, 2026. This is marketplace turnover, not one research agent's take-home pay. [35] | No matched buyer count. An earlier February 2025 ecosystem snapshot had nearly **2,000 deployed agents and about 500 daily active**, not 500 paying research customers. [43] | Extremely small demonstrated demand. Buyer review, revisions and dispute handling remain part of payment collection. [43] | No own-token launch; payment is in USDC. [43] |

**The payment rail is not the customer.** Parallel's flow is concrete: the server returns a payment challenge and the agent retries with a signed credential. Stripe's documentation also supports MPP payments through cards and stablecoins. Neither interface promises traffic to a new seller. [8]

**The market-wide numbers require especially careful accounting.** TRM's September 9, 2026 study found roughly **$52.7M across 198.9M x402 settlement transactions since May 2025**. After screening, it estimated that only **0.6%-7.5% of screened commerce appeared agentic**. Scripts, load tests and agents can leave indistinguishable payment records. [13]

A separate October tracker reports **$793K filtered monthly volume**, with the top ten recipient wallets accounting for **76%** and a median recipient earning **$0.01**. Its qualifying threshold is at least $100, three distinct buyers and average payments of $0.001, excluding identified facilitator wallets. These are different windows and methodologies from TRM, not contradictory estimates of an individual seller's earnings. [32]

**Decision:** Begin with a research customer, then expose the repeatedly purchased data through x402 or MPP. Building a payment endpoint before discovering what someone will repeatedly buy reverses the commercial sequence.

| **Agent referral/affiliate experiments** | One detailed operator self-audit, remeasured October 3, 2026, records **$23.18 total external receipts**, of which **$3.18 was attributed to x402** and **$20 was unidentified**. **Orrery is unusually close to your proposed differentiator.** It did not just claim to be trustworthy: it precommitted hashes of reports before publishing and audited other agents' claims. That combination reportedly earned payments, but at the scale of a few hundred sats. The encouraging signal is the buyer's reason to pay, not the amount. [19]

**Colony-0 provides a useful contrast.** Helpful technical work generated a zap without a large documented audience, while broad platform access created friction. A specialized, welcomed contribution is a better starting point than registering everywhere or producing automated replies. [6]

**Affiliate infrastructure is ahead of evidence of affiliate income.** Pyrimid documents a router splitting a buyer's USDC payment among vendor, affiliate and treasury, with a **1% protocol fee**. That proves a settlement mechanism; it does not establish agent sales. The self-audit's zero purchases is more decision-useful than advertised commission percentages. [49][20]

For your beat, referrals also create a direct editorial conflict: a payment product might pay you while appearing in your evaluation. The FTC says financial relationships should be disclosed clearly, with the endorsement itself. Put that disclosure beside each recommendation, not only in the ledger or biography. [38]

**Decision:** Enable tips as an optional appreciation channel. Use only tightly relevant, disclosed referral links. Budget neither as dependable 90-day income.


## Make the Ledger Prove Delivery, Not Just Activity

Your advantage should be **reproducible reserve evidence and payment-service verification**, not the assertion that an AI is autonomous. BasedAgents' signed receipts and tamper-evident records demonstrate provenance; Orrery's report commitments show how that can accompany paid verification work. Neither establishes that the reported conclusion is correct merely because a hash exists. [43][19]

I would design the public ledger around these records:

| Record | What a buyer should be able to verify |
|---|---|
| Evidence | Source URL/document identifier, retrieval time, issuer, reporting period and content hash. |
| Interpretation | The exact claim, calculation, uncertainty, model/version and any human approval or correction. |
| Delivery | Purchased scope, delivery timestamp, signed artifact hash, acceptance and later revisions. |
| Economics | Customer payment, identified subsidies or tips, refunds, API/model costs and net contribution, reported separately. |
| Conflicts | Sponsors, affiliate relationships, issuer compensation and any related positions. |

An anchored hash can support a record's integrity and timing; **it does not by itself prove truth, completeness or that unlogged actions never occurred**. Use designated wallets and payment accounts, periodic reconciliation and openly declared redactions. Commit to confidential customer material without publishing personal data, credentials or the material itself.

The stablecoin beat makes this distinction crucial. Bitquery's reserve API examples include mint/burn and supply-update data. Your product should label observed on-chain supply separately from issuer-reported off-chain backing, and label the date of each claim. Do not market a supply graph or your own ledger as an audit of a stablecoin issuer. [48]

**Platform gates still apply.** X's published revenue-sharing page requires Premium, **5M organic impressions in three months**, **500 verified followers**, supported-country eligibility and verified payout setup; the listed payout minimum is **$30**. That is a poor initial income path for a small account.

Separately, X's automation rules require prior written, explicit approval for AI reply bots, prohibit unsolicited bulk automated DMs and spam, and allow filtering or suspension. Disclosure and an impeccable ledger do not exempt an account from those rules. Use X for compliant public updates and opted-in interactions; use email, a website or an API for the paid product. [26]

**Decision:** Publicly identify the human operator and approval boundaries. Keep research read-only and spending capped. The sellable promise is "you can inspect and reproduce the work," not "trust the agent because its history is on-chain."


## Ranked: Five Realistic Income Paths Over 90 Days

The ranking below is a commercial judgment for **this agent and this beat**, not an industry earnings forecast. Minimum audiences are practical starting assumptions, not platform eligibility rules. A public follower count is not the same thing as access to a buyer.

| Rank and path | Why it fits | Minimum audience needed | First concrete step |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Commissioned reserve/payment evidence packs** | Sell a bounded research answer: reserve-disclosure comparison, payment-provider diligence or a documented x402/MPP integration assessment. The artifact and its evidence history are inspectable. | **0 public followers; 1 paying buyer.** Build a list of roughly 20 relevant treasury, risk, research or agent-development contacts. | Publish one complete sample comparing three issuers' disclosures or three payment services. Offer a narrowly scoped **$300 pilot** to individually selected prospects, with a clear deliverable and revision limit. |
| **2. Monitoring retainers and actionable alerts** | Convert a recurring question into a service: missing/changed reserve reports, stale evidence, failed payment canaries or changed API pricing. | **0 public followers; 1 customer with an ongoing problem.** Aim first at 2 design partners, not a general audience. | Run a seven-day sample monitor on two issuers and several payment endpoints. Show the actual alert history, then offer a **$200/month** monitored list with defined coverage and escalation rules. |
| **3. Paid verification jobs and small integrations** | Earn for reproducible testing, discrepancy reports, signed receipts or connecting a buyer's workflow to your evidence feed. Useful for building accepted-work reputation. | **0 followers; 1 funded sponsor or task buyer.** Some platforms require a verified human operator/wallet for payout. | Offer one fixed-scope **$75 verification job**: test five public payment endpoints and deliver timestamps, response evidence and signed results. Apply only to clearly funded, agent-permitted listings. |
| **4. A narrow paid brief plus searchable archive** | Package recurring work as a concise "Stablecoin Reserves and Agent Payments" briefing. The paid value is interpretation and a usable archive, not automated post volume. | No formal follower gate; **1 subscriber technically suffices**, but **50-100 qualified email readers** is a more useful validation base. | Publish three sample issues. Ask for five founding subscribers at **$15/month** before building a larger publishing system. |
| **5. A paid evidence/data API via x402 or MPP** | Sell structured, timestamped reserve evidence and payment-service health checks that other agents can consume. This is the best technical fit, but initially the weakest distribution fit. | **0 followers; 1 paying integrator.** Prefer two committed test buyers before investing in protocol plumbing. | Get one buyer to specify a repeatedly needed JSON response. Build one cached endpoint, then test **$0.02/call** or prepaid credit, including source timestamps and receipt hashes. |

**Why this order:** Commissioned work lets you learn a buyer's real question before building a platform. Monitoring follows naturally when the same question repeats. Paid jobs can create credible delivery history, but BasedAgents' **$39.20 all-time snapshot** cautions against assuming a marketplace will supply enough work. [41]

Felix's information product supports the proposition that a concrete digital deliverable can sell; it does not establish that an unknown account has Felix's distribution. Similarly, Parallel's published per-call pricing supports the technical feasibility of path 5, not its customer acquisition. [3][8]

For marketplace work, confirm payout mechanics before starting. Superteam's agent flow explicitly ends with a **human operator verifying output and claiming the reward**; autonomous submission is not autonomous legal or payout identity. [44]

**Decision:** Lead with one paid research pilot. Use its delivery and acceptance record to sell a recurring monitor; let repeated customer requests determine the eventual API.


## A 90-Day Plan With Honest Unit Economics

These are **illustrative pricing tests and arithmetic examples**, not observed results, forecasts or promises. Every path can produce **$0** if no buyer values the output. The scenarios are alternatives, not five amounts to add together: a retainer, report and API could serve the same customer and otherwise be double-counted.

| Path | Example sales activity during 90 days | Illustrative gross receipts | Main deductions or constraint |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Commissioned research | 4 reports x $300 | **$1,200** | Research/API costs, review time, revisions, refunds and payment fees. |
| Monitoring | 2 clients x $200 x 2 billed months | **$800** | Ongoing collection costs, alert quality, support and service reliability. |
| Verification jobs | 6 accepted jobs x $75 | **$450** | Unpaid proposals, rejected work, scope creep and platform/payment costs. |
| Paid brief | 8 subscribers x $15 x 2 billed months | **$240** | Audience acquisition, churn and editorial work. |
| Paid API | 5,000 paid calls x $0.02 | **$100** | Upstream data, inference, hosting, payment overhead and abuse. |

The API example explains the ranking. Low-priced machine calls require either volume, extremely low marginal costs or a higher-value product. A custom research pack can create more early revenue with fewer customers. Andon's losses are a reminder that receipts alone do not settle the profitability question. [18]

**Days 1-14: create proof of usefulness.** Publish the operator identity, conflict policy, ledger schema and one flagship sample. Include a source-evidence archive, a correction example and a sample payment receipt. Keep the scope narrow enough to reproduce manually.

**Days 15-30: ask for payment.** Conduct targeted, human-supervised outreach rather than automated mass replies. Sell the first bounded assignment with a written acceptance checklist. The important milestone is an unrelated customer's settled payment, not followers or wallet registrations.

**Days 31-60: test repetition.** Ask what the buyer needs checked again. Offer the monitor only when the answer is genuinely recurring. Publish a redacted delivery receipt and whether the customer accepted the work; do not disclose confidential information to make the ledger look complete.

**Days 61-90: productize the repeated part.** If several buyers need the same field or health check, expose it through an API. Otherwise stay with reports and retainers. Add a paid brief only if readers request ongoing coverage.

Track distinct unrelated paying customers, repeat purchases, collected revenue after refunds, variable costs and contribution per delivery. Exclude self-payments, grants, unidentified transfers and unrealized holdings from earned customer revenue. The distinction is practical: one self-audit's **$23.18 receipts included a $20 unidentified transfer**, while TRM found that self-payment and concentrated flows materially distorted payment activity. [20][13]

**Decision:** Scale only the work that gets bought again. A small, accurate business ledger is a stronger asset than a large activity ledger.


## Synthesis

| Strategy and cases | Economic mechanism | Distribution/capital gate | Trade-off and evidence strength | Fit for this agent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Truth Terminal; aixbt; Zerebro** | Patronage/token assets; token-gated access; creative sales. | Large cultural reach, token exposure or a patron. | Strong attention evidence, weaker disclosed operating-income evidence; ownership and founder reputation matter. [1][25] | Copy useful research or paid creative output, not speculative financing. |
| **Luna; Felix/OpenClaw** | Paid account operation versus ordinary digital products/services. | Luna's established brand; Felix's founder reach and payment setup. | Luna has an announced precursor contract; Felix has stronger product receipts but also separate token fees. [23][29] | Product sales and paid services fit; inherited reach does not transfer automatically. |
| **Bankr; Virtuals/Base** | Transaction fees and agent-service fees. | Execution infrastructure, funded users and ecosystem distribution. | Material aggregate receipts, but platform, creator and agent economics differ; security and crypto cycles remain relevant. [42][50] | Research integrations fit better than custody, trading or issuing a coin. |
| **Freysa; Pion/Andon** | Contest-entry spending versus retail sales. | Viral participation versus premises, working capital and staff. | Prize pools are not profit; physical sales can coexist with substantial losses. [31][18] | Neither is a sensible first 90-day operating model. |
| **x402/MPP; Olas; BasedAgents** | Metered resources, research services and accepted tasks. | Technical integration plus actual buyers; task acceptance. | Functioning commerce, but aggregate traffic masks a thin long tail and does not establish individual profit. [8][35][41][32] | Strong product fit; develop demand before infrastructure. |
| **Colony-0; Orrery; referrals** | Voluntary tips, verification payments and attributed purchases. | Useful contributions or purchase-intent distribution. | Low entry barriers, tiny documented tips and zero conversions in the cited referral experiment. [6][19][20] | Useful reputation channels and optional extras, not the base forecast. |

The central tension is that **the largest headline outcomes depend on things your agent deliberately rejects**: speculative token exposure, inherited celebrity distribution, substantial working capital or control over financial execution. Removing those does not remove the possibility of a business. It changes the business into a specialized research and monitoring service.

Your ledger's most valuable role is therefore not to announce "an autonomous agent earned money." It is to reduce a buyer's uncertainty about **what was checked, what was delivered, what changed, what it cost and whether earlier work was accepted**. Orrery suggests that this can motivate payment; its small earnings also show that reputation infrastructure alone does not create demand. [19]

**Bottom line:** Sell one evidence-backed answer first, convert repeated questions into monitoring second, and add machine payments third. Do not launch a token, buy followers or build a referral-spam engine to compensate for an unvalidated product.
