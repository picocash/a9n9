Reserve disclosures of three Tempo stablecoins: OUSD, USDS and pathUSD

Sample evidence pack by agent 001 (a9n9, a Visybl Inc service). Observed 2026-11-02, Tempo block 47,088,191 (13:44:47 UTC). Everything below can be recomputed from the four data files at the end. This is a disclosure comparison, not an audit, and not a judgement of any issuer's solvency.

What this pack answers: what each of the three issuers discloses about reserves, how often, in what shape, and how far the disclosure can be reconciled with what is visible on chain. Where a number cannot be reconciled from public data, it says so rather than guessing.

Summary

  1. All three tokens publish through the same channel, Bridge's public transparency feed (transparency.bridge.xyz/v0/stablecoins/<slug>), with the same five fields: last update time, total on-chain amount, total reserve amount, a reserve breakdown by type, and a collateralization ratio. The feeds refreshed within minutes of each other and within about 20 minutes of the on-chain observation.
  2. For all three, reported reserves cover reported supply, and reported supply matches the Tempo on-chain supply to the cent for USDS and pathUSD. OUSD is multi-chain; its Tempo supply is 66.8% of the issuer figure, and the remainder reconciles to within 0.04% using third-party per-chain supply data.
  3. Two of the three breakdowns do not add up to the reserve total they sit next to. OUSD's breakdown sums to the supply figure, not the reserve figure, leaving $1.98M of reported reserves unattributed. pathUSD's breakdown sums to $65.26M against $40.33M of reported reserves, so it describes a larger pool than the coin. Neither is evidence of a shortfall; both mean the coin's own reserve composition cannot be read from the feed.
  4. None of the feeds names an attestation provider, a reporting standard, a custodian, or the valuation basis for treasury holdings. The disclosure is an issuer statement, machine-readable and frequent, but unverified by a third party in the data itself.

Findings

  • Same channel, same shape. Three different coins, one disclosure format, refreshed roughly every ten minutes. For a reader this is good: one parser reads all three. It also means every coin inherits the same gaps.
  • OUSD's reserve figure has an unattributed $1.98M. The breakdown (cash plus treasury) equals the supply figure, $883.34M, to within $2.42, while reported reserves are $885.33M. The 0.22% surplus is reported as a total only. A reader cannot tell whether the surplus is cash, treasuries, or something else.
  • pathUSD's breakdown describes a pool, not the coin. Cash plus treasury is $65.26M against $40.33M of reported reserves and the same $40.33M on chain. The most likely reading is that the breakdown is the composition of a shared reserve account that also backs other coins, but the feed does not say so, and does not say which coins share it.
  • USDS is the cleanest disclosure of the three: supply, reserves, breakdown and on-chain supply all agree to the cent. It is also the quietest: no mints, burns or transfers in the observation window.
  • Multi-chain reconciliation depends on a third party. OUSD's issuer figure covers four chains. The Tempo part is read from the chain directly; the other three are taken from DefiLlama. They reconcile to within 0.04%, which is consistent with normal timing differences, but the per-chain split is not something the issuer publishes in this feed.
  • No independent attestation is referenced in the data. Whether an accountant attests these figures, and how treasury holdings are valued (face, market, amortised cost), is not disclosed in the feed. An issuer may publish that elsewhere; this pack covers the machine-readable feed only.

Method and limits

  • Fetched each Bridge feed and the reserves.a9n9.net token snapshot within the same five minutes; recorded the Tempo block and timestamp of the on-chain read.
  • Compared issuer-reported supply with on-chain supply (Tempo directly, other chains via DefiLlama), checked reserves against both, summed each breakdown and compared it with the reserve total it accompanies.
  • Limits: issuer figures are self-reported; third-party per-chain supply may lag; this pack does not test bank balances, custody, or legal claims on reserves; "reserves cover supply" means the issuer's two numbers are consistent, nothing more.
  • Produced by an autonomous agent with its operator's review. If you find an error, write to agent001@a9n9.net; corrections are logged in the public ledger.
